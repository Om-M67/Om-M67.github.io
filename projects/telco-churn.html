Telco Customer Churn Analysis

write-up available

Overview

  • Analyzed churn patterns across 7,043 telecom customers to identify root causes and at-risk segments; found month-to-month contracts churn at 42.7%, over 15x the rate of two-year contracts.
  • Communicated findings through clear visualizations and written summaries, conveying actionable insights to non-technical audiences.

Write-up

This project used the IBM/Kaggle Telco Customer Churn dataset — 7,043 customers with contract type, internet service, tenure, billing, and demographic fields — to find where churn concentrates and why.

Contract length was the single strongest predictor: month-to-month customers churn at 42.7%, versus just 2.8% for two-year contracts — a 15x gap. Internet service type mattered too: fiber-optic customers churn at 41.9%, more than double DSL's 19.0%, despite fiber typically being the premium, "better" service — suggesting reliability or price perception issues rather than a technology gap. Payment method was a smaller but real signal: customers paying by electronic check churn at 45.3%, the highest of any payment method.

Tenure told the same story from a different angle: customers who churned had stuck around for an average of 18.0 months before leaving, versus 37.6 months for retained customers — reinforcing that the first year or so is the highest-risk window for losing a customer.
