Netflix Analysis

write-up available

Overview

  • Analyzed a dataset of 8,807 Netflix titles (2008–2021) to uncover content trends across genres, countries, and content types; identified a peak in 2019 followed by a COVID-related production slowdown in 2020–2021.
  • Discovered that 69.6% of Netflix's catalog is movies and that TV-MA is the most common content rating with 3,207 titles, communicating findings through targeted visualizations.

Write-up

This project used the Netflix Movies and TV Shows dataset (as of 2021) — 8,807 titles with type, country, rating, and date-added metadata — to trace how the catalog grew and shifted over time.

Movies dominate the catalog at 69.6% versus TV shows, and TV-MA is by far the most common content rating at 3,207 titles — signaling a catalog skewed toward mature-audience content. The United States supplies the largest share of titles (3,211), followed by India (1,008) and the United Kingdom (628), reflecting Netflix's biggest production and licensing markets.

Titles-added-per-year peaked in 2019 at 2,016 new titles, then dropped to 1,879 in 2020 (a 6.8% decline) and continued falling to 1,498 in 2021 — consistent with COVID-19 production shutdowns disrupting new content pipelines industry-wide, rather than any single decision by Netflix.
