Duffing Oscillator Simulation and Fourier Analysis

write-up available

Overview

  • Implemented a numerical solution using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method, demonstrating ability to reason through and debug complex algorithmic logic.
  • Applied Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) to study frequency components; developed scripts utilizing NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib for efficient computation and analysis.

Write-up

The Duffing oscillator is a classic nonlinear differential equation used to model systems with a restoring force that isn't simply proportional to displacement — think a stiffening spring, a driven pendulum, or certain electrical circuits. Unlike a simple harmonic oscillator, the Duffing equation can produce chaotic behavior: small changes in initial conditions or driving parameters can send the system down wildly different trajectories over time.

Since the equation has no general closed-form solution, this project solved it numerically using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method (RK4) — a standard technique for integrating ordinary differential equations that balances accuracy against computational cost by sampling the system's rate of change at four points within each time step rather than just one.

With the time-domain solution in hand, a Discrete Fourier Transform was applied to shift the signal into the frequency domain — revealing which oscillation frequencies dominate the motion and how that spectrum changes as the driving force is tuned from periodic into chaotic regimes. This combination (numerical integration + frequency analysis) is a common toolkit in computational physics for studying nonlinear dynamical systems more broadly, beyond just this one equation.
